# fi-engine: peer review against the platform brief, 3 Nov 2026

The fixed income backtester that now lives in `engine/`, reviewed as a senior quantitative engineer
would review a system offered as "a solid and generic backtester for fixed income": against the
platform brief that defined it, and against its own code.

**What was read.** The brief in full: the 18-section report ("Institutional fixed income simulation
platform": scope, modes, runtime semantics, domain objects, data, accounting, policies and
execution, valuation, structured products, configuration, compute, validation, procurement,
delivery), the structured-credit acceptance matrix (F01–F25), the procurement demonstrations
(P1–P12) and the report's own fixtures (R1–R15). The brief stays private; this page paraphrases it
and cites it by section (§) and case. The engine: all nine
crates (20,700 lines of Rust), the Python package and its tests (2,200 lines), the examples and
the spec.

**What was run.** The engine's whole suite in its new place: 120 Rust tests and 80 Python tests
pass. Probes, in [`engine-repro/`](engine-repro), each a script that shows one finding.

## Verdict

**A strong economic kernel, not yet a fixed income backtester.** The parts that are hardest to
retrofit are right:
- every input is a bitemporal observation, and point in time holds by construction (I2, I9);
- a unit's state is shared and holdings are quantities, so P&L splits by role through one identity
  that is checked every step (I1);
- a book is a contract, so a deal's waterfall, a fund's sleeves and a portfolio are one mechanism;
- the ledger knows trade date from settlement date, receivables, dues, margin calls, financing
  agreements, borrow, investor flows and a horizon;
- run modes (replay, scenario, counterfactual, simulation) follow from what a run substitutes;
- unsupported features are refused unless the run declares an approximation.

That is the brief's §4–§7 core, and it is better than most commercial engines manage.

**But an instrument cannot be defined by its contract.** There are four contract types: a vendor
total-return level, a bond whose marks *and* cash flows are supplied, a cash account and a
daily-settled future. A bond's coupon, accrued interest, amortisation, call, default and recovery
must all arrive as data, and nothing checks them against each other (C1). There is no day count,
no holiday calendar, no schedule, no curve and no price-from-yield (C2, C3). So the engine can
replay supplied histories faithfully, but it cannot:
- pay a coupon it was not told about;
- tell a coupon from the accrued interest that should have paid it (H1);
- value a bond off a curve in a scenario, or a forward world;
- call, put, extend, default or exchange a security.

The engine's own acceptance map agrees: 16 of the brief's 52 cases wait for this "debt core", and
most of the 9 partial ones do too. Nautilus and Polars are used well where used, but narrowly
(H5), and the engine is not yet connected to the watcher it now lives beside (H6).

### C1: an instrument cannot be defined by its contract

The brief's product catalogue (§2) asks for 24 families: sovereigns, inflation-linked debt,
covered bonds, corporates with calls and make-wholes, hybrids, structured notes, loans, MBS and
TBA, CLOs, ABS, CMBS, derivatives, CDS, repo. Each needs "coupon schedules, settlement
conventions, ex-coupon rules", "fixed and floating coupons, calls, puts, make-whole terms", "rate
fixings, lookbacks, lockouts, floors" (§2, the matrix). §5 asks for a product adapter that can
"resolve terms, enumerate relevant events, apply a contractual event, calculate expected cash
flows, value an instrument".

The engine's contracts (`crates/fi-std/src/contracts.rs`, whose own header says "None needs terms:
richer definitions … come with the debt core in Phase 3"):

| Contract | What it knows |
| --- | --- |
| `TotalReturn` | A level series; all P&L is price |
| `SuppliedBond` | Supplied clean and accrued marks, and supplied flows (interest, principal, write-down, write-up, PIK) |
| `CashAccount` | A market-wide rate |
| `DailyFuture` | A settlement price, as variation margin |

So every bond calculation the brief lists is the data provider's. A researcher with an issuance
calendar and a terms sheet, which is what a bond desk has, cannot run a backtest until someone
else has turned them into daily accrued interest and dated cash flows; and a forward simulation
cannot pay the coupons of a bond it has priced. The engine's acceptance map lists 16 cases
blocked on this alone (F08–F11, F13, F15–F17, F19; R3, R5, R11, R13; P3, P4, P8), and it is
behind most of the 9 partial ones (F04, F12, F22, P7, P9).

**Fix.** A debt core: one generic contract built from terms, not a type per product. Its parts are
legs (a balance, an accrual rule, a payment schedule), conventions, rights and credit events, each
a definition the user composes. It is described under "How it becomes great".

### C2: no conventions

Every interest calculation hard-codes its day count where it is written:
- ACT/360: cash deposits and borrowing, borrow fees and financing agreements (`ledger.rs:1034`),
  a tranche's accrual and a deal's fees (`structured.rs:128,164`), the cash account
  (`contracts.rs:295`);
- ACT/365.25: the simulated loans in `worlds.rs:93`.

`time.rs` has a calendar of observation times and `Calendar::weekdays`. It has no holidays,
business-day conventions, schedule generation, stubs, end-of-month rules, payment lags, ex-coupon
periods or record dates. Gilts trade ex-dividend seven business days before a coupon, US
Treasuries are ACT/ACT ICMA, sterling money markets ACT/365F and euro corporates ACT/ACT ICMA on
a TARGET calendar. None of that can be stated, so any of it is either supplied as data or wrong.
The brief's first acceptance case for sovereigns is "coupon entitlement across a settlement
boundary" (§2); R1 passes only because the coupon's date and amount are supplied.

**Fix.** Conventions as definitions: day counts (ACT/360, ACT/365F, ACT/ACT ISDA and ICMA, the
30/360 family), calendars from declared holidays and rules, business-day adjustment, schedules
with stubs, payment lags and ex-coupon periods. The kernel's own interest calculations take their
day count from the agreement that defines them.

### C3: no valuation or risk

§9 asks for a typed analytics boundary returning prices, cash flows and risks with their
provenance, and §5 for risk identifiers "with economic definitions": "Dollar PV01, percentage
price change per basis point, zero-curve sensitivity, and market-quote sensitivity cannot be
interchanged … Store the bump definition, units, curve and tenor, reporting currency, aggregation
rule". The engine has a Nelson–Siegel fit and a Merton model as examples, and nothing that values
a cash flow: no curve object, no discounting, no yield or spread solver, no duration, no bump. So:
- static scenario revaluation (§3) can only shock supplied marks, not reprice;
- a forward world (§3, E3) prices a bond only through a model written for the example;
- attribution cannot split curve from spread (M7);
- an optimiser could not constrain duration or spread duration (§8).

**Fix.** Curves (discount and projection, from observed zero rates or discount factors, declared
interpolation), PV of the terms' remaining flows, clean and dirty price, yield to maturity and
Z-spread solvers, and DV01, CS01 and key-rate sensitivities by bump-and-reprice, each carrying its
bump definition (§5).

### H1: supplied accrued interest and coupons are never reconciled (reproduced)

[`p1_coupon_counted_twice.py`](engine-repro/p1_coupon_counted_twice.py): one bond, held 60 days,
accruing 0.01 per 100 a day, paying 0.30 on day 30.

| Supplied accrued interest | Income | Identity error |
| --- | --- | --- |
| resets on the coupon date | 590,000.00 | 3.0e-16 |
| never resets | **890,000.00** | 3.0e-16 |

Income is ΔA + coupon, so a series whose accrual does not fall back books the coupon twice, and
the identity balances because it holds for any inputs. The brief's §7: "Accrued interest and
coupon cash must transfer consistently so the coupon is not counted twice." With terms, both come
from one schedule and cannot disagree. For supplied histories, which replay needs, the bond should
check that accrued interest falls by the coupon on its date, within a tolerance, and fail or
report otherwise (I8).

### H2: no holding transitions

The spec's axiom 5 says a holding's quantity changes "through a trade … or through an explicit
transition of the holding", but the kernel has no transition: nothing turns one unit of a
defaulted bond into a recovery claim, a called bond into cash at the call price, an exchanged
sovereign into new bonds plus cash plus a contingent instrument (§2, EM debt; R3; P3), or a
convertible into shares. Inside a deal, claims are redeemed and replaced (F04), but a book cannot
do it for the securities it holds.

**Fix.** A transition in the ledger: at an event, each unit of A becomes a set of (entity,
quantity) and cash, priced and journalled once, with the P&L booked as principal or recovery, and
with the event idempotent at the ledger boundary (§5).

### H3: the ledger is one object, and its order is fixed

`ledger.rs` is 2,172 lines: one `Ledger` with about fifty methods running every step in one order:
contract flows, interest, weight resets, investor flows, decisions, submission, fills, then
settlement, dues, margin calls, agreements, responses, cash checks, constraints, distribution and
liquidation. Two consequences:
- **Ordering is an assumption nobody can see or vary.** The brief: "For events sharing a
  timestamp, a documented priority and stable identifier make replay deterministic … When
  historical timestamps cannot resolve ordering, expose the assumption and test economically
  material alternatives" (§4). Here a redemption, a margin call and a sale settling on the same
  day always resolve in the order the method calls happen to run.
- **Every new obligation touches the object.** Financing, margin, distributions, borrow and
  settlement share state through one struct, so a change to one is reviewed against all.

**Fix.** The ledger split into its phases (flows and interest, investor flows, decisions and
orders, execution, settlement and dues, financing, checks and constraints, distribution, output),
each in a module of its own, with the order of a step written once as a declared, documented
sequence the journal records.

### H4: fixed point in name only

The spec: "Cash and flows are fixed-point decimals, as i128 with 16 decimal places … so I6 holds
exactly", and I1 "cash to the smallest unit of the currency". But a contract's flows are `f64` per
unit (`Flows { cash: [f64; 8] }`), the ledger multiplies them by `f64` quantities and FX rates,
and converts the product (`Cash::from_f64(settled_q * unit)`, `ledger.rs` contract events). The
balances then add exactly, but the amounts added are binary approximations of what the contract
pays. A coupon of 2.375% on 50,000,000 face is exact in decimal and inexact in `f64`. The error is
far below a cent at such sizes, but the guarantee the spec states is not the one the code
provides, and contracts that round (deals) round in `Cash` while bonds do not.

**Fix.** Contractual amounts computed in decimal from the terms (rate × face × year fraction,
rounded as the contract says), carried as `Cash`, and multiplied by quantity in decimal; `f64`
only for valuations and measures, as the spec says.

### H5: Nautilus, narrowly

What is used, and used well:
- `nautilus_model::orderbook::OrderBook` for fills against a simulated L2 book (`fi-exec`);
- Nautilus's currency registry for minor units (`fi_exec::rounding`);
- `nautilus-analysis` for performance statistics (`fi-metrics`).

What is not, though Nautilus is the established implementation:
- exchange-traded hedges (bond and rate futures, FX) are not Nautilus instruments, so their
  multipliers, tick sizes, activation and expiry are re-specified by hand, and a contract roll (R4,
  P9) cannot be expressed;
- the order book fills only research orders; Nautilus's fee and fill models, and its matching of
  order types, are not used;
- the spec's I4 (fidelity parity) and I6 (Nautilus's quantities and cash equal the ledger's after
  every event) name an "Implement" driver on Nautilus that does not exist.

**Fix.** Exchange-traded contracts defined by Nautilus's `FuturesContract` (and currency pairs for
FX), rolls between them as a policy, fills through the order book with Nautilus's types at the
boundary, and the spec's invariants stated as what is built.

### H6: not connected to the watcher

The engine runs its ledger in Rust with Python's lock released, which is right, but nothing
reports progress while it does, and its results (the ledger frame, orders, fills, the journal, the
manifest) are Polars frames in memory rather than kept artifacts. A run under `gongoozler run` is
a black box until it ends, and two runs cannot be compared with `gongoozler diff`. Its examples are
scripts for its own virtual environment, not run files.

**Fix.** The engine's loops write progress into the run's ring (spec/ring.md) from Rust, at no
measurable cost; a driver keeps its results as canonical artifacts with a manifest; its examples
become run files; one build and one CI.

### Medium and low

- **M1, M2.** Split `structured.rs` into terms, waterfall, tests and triggers, rights and manager;
  split the bindings by object. `Strategy`'s options group into the objects they configure (cash,
  shorts, financing, execution, horizon).
- **M3.** Rates per currency, each with its day count and compounding.
- **M4.** Money-weighted return and IRR beside the time-weighted return, labelled as answering a
  different question (§7).
- **M5, M6, M7.** Auction and primary-allocation fills; an optimiser behind the decision
  interface with solver status and binding constraints (§8); attribution by curve, spread, carry
  and roll-down once analytics exist (§7).
- **M8.** The full-scale budget run in CI, where memory allows (the machine this was reviewed on
  has 1.4 GB free).
- **L1.** The grader's adapter moves out of the engine's crates into a tool.
- **L2.** The spec states what is built and marks what is planned; design history stays private.

## Coverage against the brief

**The 52 cases.** The engine's own map counts 27 complete, 9 partial and 16 waiting for the debt
core. The named tests exist and pass; this review did not re-derive each case's numbers, and the
map is the engine's own claim.

**The product families (§2).** No family can be defined from its terms. Families that replay from
supplied data today: nominal sovereigns and corporates (supplied flows), agency MBS factors
(supplied), CLO debt and equity and ABS (the deal model), repo (agreements), futures (variation
margin). Not representable: inflation-linked debt, callable and make-whole bonds, floaters with
fixings, hybrids, convertibles, municipals' sinking funds, loans' facilities and draws, TBA, CMBS
servicing, MSRs, SRT credit events, CDS, swaps.

**Modes (§3).** Replay, policy backtest, counterfactual and forward simulation are built; static
scenario revaluation needs pricing (C3); screening and shadow operation are not.

## How it becomes great

Keep the kernel. Build the fixed income on it, in Rust, generic:

1. **Conventions** (`fi-terms`): day counts, calendars, business-day adjustment, schedules with
   stubs and lags, ex-coupon and record dates. Data the user declares; nothing about a country.
2. **Legs**: a balance (face, amortisation schedule, sinking fund, factor, PIK, write-downs), an
   accrual rule (fixed, step, floating on a fixing series with spread, gearing, cap, floor,
   lookback, lockout and compounding in arrears, inflation-indexed with lag, interpolation and a
   deflation floor, zero) and a payment schedule.
3. **Rights**: calls and puts on schedules (with make-whole at a spread over a curve), sinking
   funds, extensions (soft bullets), conversions, exercised either as observed events (replay) or
   by a declared rule or model (counterfactual and simulation).
4. **Credit**: default, recovery and exchange as events that move holdings (H2): claims extinguished
   and created once.
5. **One debt contract** that steps all of the above for a unit, on the kernel's `Contract` trait,
   computing coupons and accrued interest in decimal (H4) and valuing from marks in a declared
   quotation basis (price, yield, spread) or from a pricing model.
6. **Analytics**: curves, PV, clean and dirty price, yield and spread solvers, DV01, CS01 and
   key-rate sensitivities by declared bumps, usable in a run and on their own.
7. **Nautilus where it is the established implementation**: its instruments for exchange-traded
   contracts and rolls, its order book and types for fills, its statistics.
8. **The watcher**: progress from Rust into the ring, results kept, examples as run files, one CI.

valued with fixings dated after the valuation (a look-ahead); a seasoned pool starting at its
